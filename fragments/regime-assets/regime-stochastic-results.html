Scenario reduction: WS decisions and five forecast groups

208 to 80 leaves; 13 forecast methods to 5 groups. All four observable information nodes and all four terminal regimes remain. Reduction does not merge calm and stress regimes, change an observed prefix or reveal a future method to recourse.

This removes 61.5% of leaf evaluations per candidate policy. It is a one-off WS grouping cost followed by a smaller optimisation problem; actual wall-clock speed-up is not guaranteed to equal the 2.6-fold leaf-count ratio.

The single-scenario perfect-information search found 64 distinct initial allocations and 98 distinct initial/recourse pairs across 208 leaves. These are heuristic WS candidates, not certified optimal WS solutions. Equality here describes the candidates found; it is not a proof that the underlying optimisation problems are equivalent.

How grouping works: compare each pair of forecast methods across matching node/regime cells, using the probability-weighted monetary distance between their WS initial and recourse allocations. Exhaustively examine all 1,287 choices of five representative methods. Forecast-path distance only breaks ties. The resulting groups can contain similar rather than identical decisions; this is approximate decision-based reduction.

Representative: retain an actual original method path in each node/regime cell and add the probabilities of its group members. Do not average prices: netSD dominance and technical sale gates are nonlinear. The four-by-four node/regime probability masses are preserved exactly. Original conditional sale permissions, liquidity limits, budget, integer lots and nonanticipativity remain unchanged.
