Method and practical limits

Historical score normalization: the observed reference-network denominator D is held fixed for both paired searches. A proposed portfolio can have a larger dominance degree than any reference, so this historical diagnostic can exceed +1 or -1. It is not a probability. OUT, IN and D are shown explicitly. The terminal diagnostic instead uses the theoretical maximum denominator 3R and remains between -1 and +1.

USD closes are aligned to the Baltic price calendar using only prior US quotes on US holidays; EUR valuation still uses the available date-specific ECB rate. Returns span the same Baltic sessions. No pre-inception backfill.

One expanded common network (all eligible core and US assets plus Current) per context. Both paired searches use exactly the same references and maximum observed OUT+IN denominator. Historical NETSD normalises the invested asset sleeve, consistent with the existing historical diagnostic; the terminal diagnostic instead includes residual cash in total EUR 15000 wealth. Never compare these numbers directly with the core-only network or across these two definitions.

MILP surrogate seeds followed by exact portfolio-CDF neighbourhood evaluation; heuristic, no global optimality certificate. Zero US names is feasible and three is a maximum. Tie breaks prefer fewer US names, then lower US spend.

Current SPY membership creates survivorship bias; descriptive optimisation is not an out-of-sample backtest.

Indicative ECB EUR conversion; brokerage, execution spread, taxes and cash dividends are excluded.

Regimes are retrospective HMM classifications; diagnostic observations are excluded from selection.

US sector correspondence for the shared model: Information Technology → Technology; Communication Services → Telecommunications; Materials → Basic Materials. Other GICS sector names match directly.
