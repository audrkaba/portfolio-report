Methodology, equations, model fits and interpretation
Data, dates and distinct history windows

All US series use completed Yahoo Finance USD closes, converted to EUR with dated ECB USD-per-EUR reference rates. Cash dividends are excluded, matching the existing price-return convention. The candidate selection was fixed before this analysis: the top 15 SPY companies plus up to four companies per GICS sector, one share class per company. Current membership introduces survivorship bias in historical comparisons.

Observed Baltic sessions define the shared calendar. Prices are carried forward only after an instrument's genuine inception, including exchange holidays and delayed NAV publication; no earlier observations are invented. Same-date US closes occur after Baltic closes. Stale Baltic quotes and asynchronous sessions can bias daily correlations and connectedness; these are descriptive market-risk diagnostics, not a causal contagion estimate.

The long window retains at least 1000 training plus 126 diagnostic observations. CEG determines its later common start. GEV lacks the long-history minimum and therefore appears only in the explicitly shorter comparison. The last 126 observations never fit GARCH/DCC parameters, dominance reference networks or VAR coefficients. Frozen fitted GARCH/DCC parameters are subsequently filtered through the observed diagnostic closes to obtain the latest conditional risk estimates. Missing historical regime labels are retained only in Full history; regimes use the existing retrospective labels and are not refitted by adding US equities. Small-context netSD is empirical and flagged; tail estimates and VAR are unavailable when their sample rules fail.

True netSD, dynamic normalisation, cash and portfolio comparison

Every candidate is its own empirical return distribution. Pairwise FSD, SSD and TSD are evaluated on the full combined supports with the existing exact numerical tolerances. The reference network contains the stated asset set plus the actual Current reference; the evaluated candidate is then appended. The maximum degree is recomputed in that augmented graph for every candidate.

\[G_{ij}=\sum_{k=1}^{3}\mathbf1\{X_i\succ_k X_j\},\quad d_i^{\mathrm{out}}=\sum_jG_{ij},\quad d_i^{\mathrm{in}}=\sum_jG_{ji}.\]
\[D(w)=\max_j\bigl(d_j^{\mathrm{out}}+d_j^{\mathrm{in}}\bigr),\qquad \operatorname{netSD}(w)=\frac{d_w^{\mathrm{out}}-d_w^{\mathrm{in}}}{D(w)}.\]

The primary historical comparison retains the existing convention of invested-holdings weights, excluding cash. An account-with-cash netSD is also shown, calculated directly from the account return distribution. No constituent netSD averaging is used. Original cash is preserved when holdings are marked at newer closes; account wealth is not artificially forced back to EUR 15000. Risk columns use account weights including cash, with zero cash return.

\[r_{p,t}^{\mathrm{invested}}=\sum_i\frac{q_iP_i}{\sum_jq_jP_j}r_{i,t},\qquad r_{p,t}^{\mathrm{account}}=\sum_i\frac{q_iP_i}{c+\sum_jq_jP_j}r_{i,t}.\]

These fixed initial-allocation historical evaluations are not strategy backtests. Current-regime netSD allocations are matched to their actual regime-specific quantities; other models retain their published allocations. Existing stochastic policies, EVPI/VSS, scenario-tree terminal distributions and purchase limits are not silently relabelled as refitted to this wider universe. Changing a reference set changes the score's baseline; compare 72 versus 116/117 only in the paired same-window table.

Numerical reproducibility: dominance support values use a 10⁻¹² return grid, so insignificant floating-point changes in an identical distribution cannot produce spurious strict FSD edges. The same grid is used for the narrow and expanded networks; raw price and return histories are retained separately. FSD/SSD/TSD comparisons retain the existing 10⁻¹⁰ numerical tolerance. The displayed composite-likelihood fit criterion is per pair and observation, omitting the irrelevant Gaussian constant.

Systematic exposure, expected shortfall and conditional tail proxies

Market beta is an OLS exposure to S&P 500 EUR price returns, distinct from systemic connectedness. Alpha assumes zero risk-free return. Daily VaR and ES use losses; ES accounts for the exact 5% tail probability, including a fractional quantile atom.

\[\beta_i=\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(r_i,r_M)}{\operatorname{Var}(r_M)},\quad \alpha_i^{\mathrm{ann}}=252\bigl(\overline r_i-\beta_i\overline r_M\bigr),\quad \operatorname{ES}_{95}=v+\frac{\mathbb E[(L-v)_+]}{0.05},\quad v=Q_{0.95}(L).\]

MES is the mean asset/account loss on the market's worst 5% days. At least five market-tail days are required. The conditional-market-tail difference below is an empirical ΔCoVaR-style proxy, not the canonical quantile-regression estimator: it compares the market's 5% quantile when the asset is in its bottom 5% with the market's 5% quantile when the asset is between its 40th and 60th percentiles. At least ten observations in both conditional groups are required. Positive values indicate a worse market tail in the asset's distress group.

\[\operatorname{MES}_{i,5}=-\mathbb E[r_i\mid r_M\le Q_{0.05}(r_M)],\quad \Delta_i^{\mathrm{tail}}=Q_{0.05}(r_M\mid Q_{0.4}(r_i)\le r_i\le Q_{0.6}(r_i))-Q_{0.05}(r_M\mid r_i\le Q_{0.05}(r_i)).\]

These are market-data risk proxies, not bank-capital SRISK or structural default probabilities. See Acharya et al., Measuring Systemic Risk.

Regularised VAR, generalised FEVD and directional connectedness

A 116/117-variable ordinary VAR would be unstable or unidentified in small regimes. Instead, fit a standardised ridge VAR(1), select the penalty by an 80%/20% chronological split inside training (fold scaling uses only its earlier observations), then refit to all available same-context training pairs. A pair is eligible only if both consecutive original-calendar observations belong to that regime; separated regime episodes are never joined into fictitious adjacent days. At least 40 pairs are required. Innovations use Ledoit–Wolf covariance shrinkage.

\[\widehat B_\lambda=(X^\top X+\lambda I)^{-1}X^\top Y,\qquad z_t=c+A z_{t-1}+\varepsilon_t,\quad A=\widehat B_\lambda^\top.\]

With 10-session horizon and moving-average matrices, the generalised decomposition is row-normalised. A row denotes the receiver, a column the transmitter. TO and FROM are directional sums in percentage points; they need not individually lie below 100. NET sums to zero across the network; TCI lies between 0 and 100. Predictive dependence does not establish causality.

\[\Phi_h=A^h,\quad \theta_{ij}(H)=\frac{\sigma_{jj}^{-1}\sum_{h=0}^{H-1}(e_i^\top\Phi_h\Sigma e_j)^2}{\sum_{h=0}^{H-1}e_i^\top\Phi_h\Sigma\Phi_h^\top e_i},\qquad \widetilde\theta_{ij}=100\frac{\theta_{ij}}{\sum_k\theta_{ik}}.\]
\[\mathrm{FROM}_i=\sum_{j\ne i}\widetilde\theta_{ij},\quad \mathrm{TO}_i=\sum_{j\ne i}\widetilde\theta_{ji},\quad \mathrm{NET}_i=\mathrm{TO}_i-\mathrm{FROM}_i,\quad \mathrm{TCI}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i\ne j}\widetilde\theta_{ij}.\]

See Diebold and Yilmaz, Better to Give than to Receive. This implementation applies their decomposition to a regularised return VAR; it is not presented as an unregularised replication of their volatility application.

Estimated GARCH and DCC, CPU/CUDA checks

Each return series has a demeaned Gaussian GARCH(1,1) quasi-likelihood fit. Mean and parameters use training only; positivity and persistence below 0.999 are enforced by a simplex parameterisation. GARCH residuals feed a DCC(1,1), estimated by Gaussian pairwise composite likelihood across every pair. This is explicitly a composite-likelihood estimator rather than the full multivariate Gaussian likelihood. A Ledoit–Wolf covariance target preserves a well-conditioned correlation recursion.

\[h_{i,t}=\omega_i+\alpha_i\varepsilon_{i,t-1}^2+\beta_i h_{i,t-1},\quad \omega_i>0,\quad \alpha_i,\beta_i\ge0,\quad \alpha_i+\beta_i<0.999.\]
\[Q_t=(1-a-b)\overline Q+a z_{t-1}z_{t-1}^\top+bQ_{t-1},\quad R_t=\operatorname{diag}(Q_t)^{-1/2}Q_t\operatorname{diag}(Q_t)^{-1/2}.\]
\[\ell_{\mathrm{CL}}(a,b)=\frac12\sum_t\sum_{i<j}\left[\log(1-\rho_{ij,t}^2)+\frac{z_{i,t}^2+z_{j,t}^2-2\rho_{ij,t}z_{i,t}z_{j,t}}{1-\rho_{ij,t}^2}\right].\]

Regime DCC matrices are conditional averages along the fitted full chronological training path, not separate small-sample refits. Latest/next-session DCC and GARCH filter the 126 subsequent observed closes with frozen fitted parameters. Raw returns are not winsorised or redistributed across stale-price spells; the zero-return fraction identifies possible liquidity distortion. These are statistical conditional estimates, not calibrated guarantees.

GARCH/DCC background: Engle, Dynamic Conditional Correlation; Engle, Shephard and Sheppard, Fitting Vast Dimensional Time-Varying Covariance Models. The implementation-specific regularisation, likelihood and sample choices are stated above.
